# Scout report: 21 November 2026

**Focus question from the owner:** "What improvements and features would get people hooked, so they come back and share it?"

**Short answer:** People come back to a game about *their own place* when the game shows them that it recognises their place, and when it lets them tell friends about it without giving away where they live. The best first step is cheap: make the moment of recognition deliberate ("that's my school!"). Sharing should start with a spoiler-free, location-free result card. Links that reveal a real area come last, behind a clear consent screen.

### How I judged "hooks"
I explain each idea in terms of **self-determination theory**: people keep doing what makes them feel **competent** (getting better), **autonomous** (making real choices) and **connected** (to other people). Rigby and Ryan applied it to games as the PENS model. In their studies, game preference and enjoyment were predicted by how far play met those three needs ([SDT site, PENS page](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/player-experience-of-needs-satisfaction-pens/) [search excerpt]; [Rigby & Ryan, "Rethinking Carrots", Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/feature-rethinking-carrots-measuring-player-motivation-) [search excerpt]). One 2024 review notes that games researchers have tended to treat SDT as unquestioned ([arXiv review](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.12639v1) [search excerpt]), so I use it as a lens, not as proof.

I avoided habit-loop tricks. Critics of Nir Eyal's Hook model argue that its "variable reward" step is close to how casinos keep people playing ([Axbom, "Nir Eyal's habit danger"](https://axbom.com/nir-eyal-habit-danger/) [search excerpt]). None of the ideas uses loot boxes, streaks, countdown fear of missing out, pay-to-win or nagging notifications.

## What the research says, in brief

1. **People love seeing their own place in a game, even when it isn't accurate.** In Microsoft Flight Simulator (2020), many players' first flight was over their own house and home town, and they posted the pictures ([Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/gaming/microsoft-flight-simulator-2020-locations-photos); [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/news/microsoft-flight-simulator-is-flying-high-with-over-a-million-pilots) [both search excerpts]). One guide says the street is usually recognisable even when the house itself looks generic or out of date ([Flyaway Simulation](https://flyawaysimulation.com/ask/answers/fly-over-house-flight-simulator/) [search excerpt]). A forum player wrote that their house didn't look much like their real house at first ([AVSIM forum](https://www.avsim.com/forums/topic/579620-keeping-a-sense-of-perspective/) [search excerpt]). This supports PLAN's core bet: street layout and names carry recognition, roof textures don't.
2. **The same feature got misused against real people.** Inverse reports that some Flight Simulator players flew into someone's home, took a screenshot and sent it to the person who lives there as a mean joke ([Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/gaming/microsoft-flight-simulator-2020-locations-photos) [search excerpt]). A game with a 105 mm gun over real houses must design against this from day one.
3. **Location data leaks in ways nobody planned.** Strava's public heatmap of where users exercised revealed military bases, partly because of privacy settings that users never changed ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/01/29/us-military-reviewing-tech-use-after-strava-privacy-snafu); [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2018/01/29/strava_heatmap_military_base_locations/) [both search excerpts]). Lesson: never build a public map or list of "where people play".
4. **Real-map games can damage real neighbourhoods and the real map.** Pokémon Go was sued over players trespassing to reach game spots. The settlement reportedly required removing game spots within 40 m of single-family homes on request and answering complaints within 15 days ([Law.com / The Recorder](https://law.com/therecorder/2019/02/15/pokemon-go-away-lawsuit-over-once-ubiquitous-mobile-game-settles); [PCWorld](https://www.pcworld.com/article/415906/pokmon-go-developer-sued-over-gamers-seeking-to-enter-private-property.html) [both search excerpts]). Some players also added **fake parks and beaches to OpenStreetMap** because they believed it changed where creatures appeared. OpenStreetMap's volunteers treated this as vandalism ([OSM wiki: Pokémon Go](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Go); [MDPI paper, "Cartographic Vandalism in the Era of Location-Based Games"](https://www.mdpi.com/2220-9964/9/4/197) [both search excerpts]). **This matters directly: our hordes spawn in parks, read from OpenStreetMap.**
5. **Games can make people feel more attached to their place.** Small studies of Pokémon Go players found that they discovered local spots such as parks, and that gameplay can create "place dependence" ([Karltun thesis, SLU](https://stud.epsilon.slu.se/14836/1/karltun_k_181108.pdf); [Geography of Pokémon GO, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.12041) [both search excerpts]). The second study also found the game favoured some urban areas over others, which is a fairness warning for patchy maps.
6. **Sharing works best when it spoils nothing and needs no account.** Wordle's emoji grid let people show how they did without ruining the puzzle, needed no login and copied to the clipboard. A player typed grids by hand first, and the creator then built the button into the game ([The Spinoff](https://thespinoff.co.nz/media/13-01-2022/those-coloured-boxes-on-twitter-are-new-zealands-fault); [Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/heres-why-the-word-game-wordle-went-viral-180979439/) [both search excerpts]). There was also a backlash against too many grids in people's feeds (same coverage).
7. **"Same challenge for everyone" builds a community, while dailies tied to rewards feel like chores.** In Spelunky's daily challenge everyone gets the same cave and one attempt, and players posted videos of their runs ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/now-playing-the-spelunky-daily-challenge); [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/the-understated-genius-of-the-i-spelunky-i-daily-challenge) [both search excerpts]). Kotaku argued more games need daily challenges ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/more-games-need-daily-challenge-modes-1740109783) [search excerpt]). PC Gamer argued that daily *quests* tied to rewards shift the goal from fun to box-ticking ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/daily-quests-arent-fun-theyre-tedious) [search excerpt]).
8. **Friends playing the same map at different times works.** GeoGuessr's challenge links let friends play identical rounds whenever they have time and compare scores ([TechWiser guide](https://techwiser.com/play-geoguessr-with-friends/); [GeoGuessr](https://www.geoguessr.com/) [both search excerpts]).
9. **A story and a place that grows bring people back.** In Zombies, Run!, the developers say the audio story is the main attraction for many players. Runs earn supplies that grow your base, and shared "virtual races" led to more than 200 real-world meet-ups ([Zombies, Run! blog, "Ten years"](https://blog.zombiesrungame.com/2022/03/30/ten-years-of-zombies-run/) [search excerpt]). In Death Stranding, structures other players built appear in your world and you can "like" them, a quiet kind of co-operation without playing at the same time ([PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/quiet-co-operation-with-other-players-makes-death-stranding-special); [Game Developer, Kojima interview](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/kojima-details-the-subtly-social-gameplay-of-i-death-stranding-i-) [both search excerpts]).
10. **Browser players leave if the start is slow.** Poki says browser players "haven't committed yet", so the first few seconds matter ([Poki, "What makes a high-quality browser game", May 2026](https://poki.com/blog/what-makes-high-quality-browser-game) [search excerpt]). An earlier Poki post suggests loads under 5 seconds, ideally about 3 ([Poki, April 2025](https://poki.com/blog/building-multiplayer-open-world-web-games-that-run-everywhere) [search excerpt]). Poki's 2026 player survey describes short sessions of 11–20 minutes, several times a day, and finds players tolerate less friction than before ([Poki, State of Web Gaming 2026](https://poki.com/blog/state-of-web-gaming-report-2026) [search excerpt]). CrazyGames' telemetry gives a longer average of about 30 minutes, so the sources disagree. CrazyGames advises loading what the opening needs first and the rest in the background ([CrazyGames docs](https://docs.crazygames.com/resources/getting-to-the-first-frame/) [search excerpt]). PLAN's target for your own suburb is "under 15 s", well above these numbers.
11. **Going viral could get the game blocked by its free map services.** The Nominatim address-search limit of 1 request per second is the total for *all* users of an app, not per player, and going over it leads to automatic blocks ([OSMF Nominatim usage policy](https://operations.osmfoundation.org/policies/nominatim/) [search excerpt]). Overpass limits also count per application, and the wiki advises caching ([OSM wiki: Overpass API](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Overpass_API) [search excerpt]; exact current numbers not confirmed). Any sharing feature must not make every recipient re-download a map.
12. **Genre neighbour.** Call of Duty 4's "Death From Above" gunship mission is remembered for how its thermal view recasts familiar shapes in an eerie half-light, and for the unease of telling tiny white dots apart ([GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/modern-warfares-death-from-above-mission-is-still-the-model-for-quiet-unease-in-call-of-duty) [search excerpt]). That unease is exactly why our "Protect people" and "darkly comic" pillars matter.

### 1. "Hello, home": a deliberate recognition flyover

**What the player experiences.** After the map loads, the gunship doesn't drop straight into the fight. It comes in on a 15–20 second approach in thermal. As public landmarks pass under the crosshair, the HUD labels three to five of them by their real names: "WATTLE HILL PRIMARY: SAFEHOUSE", "BANKSIA RESERVE: CONTACTS", "MAIN ST". The crew makes one dry remark aimed at the zombies, not the place. The player says "that's my school!" out loud. One key skips the flyover. Only public places are labelled. The address the player typed is never labelled or marked, and the view isn't centred on it.

**Motivation served.** *Autonomy*: it's the setting the player chose, and the game shows it noticed. *Competence*: the flyover doubles as the briefing made visible ("safehouse here, contacts there"), so the player starts the mission already oriented. *Connection*: it creates the urge to tell someone "it has my street in it", which is word-of-mouth that reveals nobody's address ("try it on yours").

**Evidence.** Flight Simulator players' first act was flying over their own homes and sharing it, and the street is recognisable even when the house isn't ([Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/gaming/microsoft-flight-simulator-2020-locations-photos), [Flyaway Simulation](https://flyawaysimulation.com/ask/answers/fly-over-house-flight-simulator/), [AVSIM](https://www.avsim.com/forums/topic/579620-keeping-a-sense-of-perspective/)). Thermal recasts familiar shapes in an eerie way ([GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/modern-warfares-death-from-above-mission-is-still-the-model-for-quiet-unease-in-call-of-duty)). Games can build attachment to local places ([Karltun](https://stud.epsilon.slu.se/14836/1/karltun_k_181108.pdf)). Browser players judge in the first seconds ([Poki](https://poki.com/blog/what-makes-high-quality-browser-game)). All [search excerpts].

**Pillar fit.** 1 (It's your place): the strongest fit of any idea. 3 (Read the picture): it shows where the threats are and introduces the thermal look. 4 (Fair): the labels repeat the briefing intel, which is always true. 5 (Tone): the jokes must target zombies and crew, never the real school or suburb. **Tension:** it adds time on top of an already long load (see idea 3), so it must be skippable and short.

**Risks.** *Privacy*: a screenshot of the flyover identifies the area. Labelling only public places and not centring on the typed address keeps the player's home out of it. *Tone*: a joke about a named real school can read as mockery, so jokes stay about the zombies. *Safety*: street and place names come from volunteers, so they go on screen with `textContent` (hard rule 4). *Scope*: low, because it reuses the POI data, briefing and orbit camera already planned.

**Smallest test.** Add this to PLAN's phase 2 five-suburb check. Have 3–5 people load their own suburb, once with the flyover and once without. Ask: "Did it feel like your place? Did you want to show someone?" Note whether they name a landmark aloud without being asked. **What proves it wrong:** most people skip it, or recognise nothing. That would also be early warning for PLAN's core assumption.

### 2. Sortie report: a result card with no location in it

**What the player experiences.** At the debrief, a "Copy sortie report" button puts a few lines of text on the clipboard, for example:

```
SPOOKY AT HOME · Get them home · Difficulty 3
Survivors home 4/4 · Friendly fire 0
Coldies found 5/6 · Cameras switched 11
Grade B+ · "Check fire. Good shooting."
```

It contains no street names, no coordinates, no map picture and no link. An optional add-on is **"Tonight's orders"**: one shared mission type, difficulty and twist each night (say, "Coldies on the march") that everyone plays on *their own* map, so cards can be compared without anyone sharing a place. There's no streak counter, nothing is lost by missing a night, and every past night's orders stay playable.

**Motivation served.** *Competence*: the card shows how well you did, and it leads with survivors saved. *Connection*: friends compare in a group chat whenever they like, with no accounts.

**Evidence.** Wordle's grid spread because it showed your result without spoiling the puzzle or needing a login ([The Spinoff](https://thespinoff.co.nz/media/13-01-2022/those-coloured-boxes-on-twitter-are-new-zealands-fault), [Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/heres-why-the-word-game-wordle-went-viral-180979439/)). A shared daily challenge built a community around Spelunky ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/now-playing-the-spelunky-daily-challenge), [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/the-understated-genius-of-the-i-spelunky-i-daily-challenge), [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/more-games-need-daily-challenge-modes-1740109783)). Dailies tied to rewards become chores ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/daily-quests-arent-fun-theyre-tedious)), so "Tonight's orders" gives no reward. Short, repeat sessions are typical on the web ([Poki 2026 report](https://poki.com/blog/state-of-web-gaming-report-2026)). All [search excerpts].

**Pillar fit.** 2 (Protect people): survivors come first on the card. 4 (Fair and repeatable): the seed makes results comparable. 5: the flavour line carries the tone. **Deliberate clash with pillar 1:** the card leaves the place out, because the place is the private part.

**Risks.** *Privacy*: low, but only if the card is built from mission numbers and never from map text. A test should confirm the card contains no OpenStreetMap strings. *Ethics*: no streaks, no "you missed last night", no countdown. *Fairness*: different maps aren't equally hard, so "Tonight's orders" should compare grades, not run a leaderboard. *Dependency*: needs the phase 3 scoring and grades. *Overuse*: Wordle shows that too many shared cards can annoy people, so keep it short.

**Smallest test.** Ship only the copy button, without "Tonight's orders", to a handful of testers. After a week, **ask them** whether they pasted it anywhere and what friends said. Don't count it with analytics, because hard rule 5 forbids that.

### 3. Play while your neighbourhood loads

**What the player experiences.** After you type an address, the crew radio starts at once and you're flying a short warm-up sortie over the sample town within seconds: a target range, or a mini "clear the horde". Meanwhile your own area downloads and the mission is built in the background. When it's ready: "Your neighbourhood is on screen." Friends who open a shared game get the same instant start. If the download fails, you're already playing, and the fallback message is calm.

**Motivation served.** *Competence*: you learn the guns and cameras before the stakes are your own streets, so your first home mission goes better. *Autonomy*: you're doing something, not watching a progress bar.

**Evidence.** Browser players drop off in the first seconds, and Poki suggests loads under 5 seconds ([Poki 2026](https://poki.com/blog/what-makes-high-quality-browser-game), [Poki 2025](https://poki.com/blog/building-multiplayer-open-world-web-games-that-run-everywhere)). Tolerance for friction has fallen ([Poki 2026 report](https://poki.com/blog/state-of-web-gaming-report-2026)). Load what the opening needs first, the rest in the background ([CrazyGames](https://docs.crazygames.com/resources/getting-to-the-first-frame/)). The free map services are shared and rate-limited, so slow or failed downloads will happen, especially when the game is popular ([Nominatim policy](https://operations.osmfoundation.org/policies/nominatim/), [Overpass wiki](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Overpass_API)). All [search excerpts]. PLAN's own target is 15 s for your suburb.

**Pillar fit.** 3 (Read the picture): it doubles as a sensor tutorial. 1: it protects the "first look at home" moment by not making people wait for it. 4: unaffected. No clash.

**Risks.** *Scope*: medium, because downloading, parsing and playing at once raises memory and timing questions for the Tech Lead. *Feel*: a warm-up that feels like homework would hurt, so let players skip to home the moment it's ready. No privacy issue.

**Smallest test.** In the playtest harness, use saved map files with a throttled network and measure **time from pressing Go to first shot fired**, with and without the warm-up. Then ask three testers whether the wait felt long.

### 4. The neighbourhood remembers (nights that carry over, stored only on your device)

**What the player experiences.** Your map becomes a place with a story over several nights. Survivors you got home last time appear as warm figures on the safehouse roof. The radio mentions streets you held ("Banksia Street's been quiet since Tuesday"). Last night's craters have cooled to cold scars in the thermal picture. The horde shifts its routes in response to where you defended hardest, in a way the briefing truthfully reports. **Nothing bad happens while you're away.** Everything is stored only in your browser, with a clear "Forget this place" button. This goes beyond the planned phase 3 campaign chain (one seed, three missions) by carrying results across separate sessions.

**Motivation served.** *Competence*: your progress is visible on the map itself. *Autonomy*: your choices about which streets to protect shape the next night. *Connection*: to the (fictional) survivors you saved, the same pull that keeps Zombies, Run! players coming back for the story.

**Evidence.** Zombies, Run!: story is the main draw, and runs grow your base ([Zombies, Run! blog](https://blog.zombiesrungame.com/2022/03/30/ten-years-of-zombies-run/)). Death Stranding: what you build persists and others see it ([PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/quiet-co-operation-with-other-players-makes-death-stranding-special)). Gameplay can create attachment to a real place ([Karltun](https://stud.epsilon.slu.se/14836/1/karltun_k_181108.pdf), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.12041)). All [search excerpts].

**Pillar fit.** 1 and 2: strong. Saved people are the reward, not kill counts. 4 (Fair and repeatable): **needs care**. The mission must be the same for the same map + seed + saved state, and the balance bot must show that every carried-over state is still winnable. 5: fine.

**Risks.** *Ethics*: it must never punish absence ("your survivors died while you were gone"). That would be guilt-tripping, which the guardrail bans. *Privacy*: the saved state contains map data about the player's area, so it stays on the device, is never uploaded, and is easy to delete. *Scope*: high (save system, generator inputs, balance coverage).

**Smallest test.** After a won escort, make the next mission on the same map mention, in its briefing, how many survivors got home and the street they walked down. No other persistence. Then ask testers whether they want to play "the next night".

### 5. Challenge-a-friend link, behind a consent screen

**What the player experiences.** After a mission, "Challenge a friend" makes a link to the exact same map, seed and mission. Your friend plays the identical sortie and both of you see each other's sortie report (idea 2). Before any link is made, a full-screen check says, in plain words: "This link shows the streets around [suburb]. Anyone you send it to, and anyone they forward it to, can see this area. It does not show your address." The player can recentre the shared mission on a public landmark (for example, the school) instead of the typed address, or share the card only. The answer is asked **every time**, with no "remember my choice", and the default is not to share. This adds guardrails to the phase 5 "shareable mission links" option and suggests building it only once ideas 1 and 2 have shown people want to share.

**Motivation served.** *Connection*: friends play the same thing on their own time. *Competence*: it's a fair head-to-head because the seed makes it identical.

**Evidence.** GeoGuessr challenge links ([TechWiser](https://techwiser.com/play-geoguessr-with-friends/)). The harassment pattern in Flight Simulator ([Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/gaming/microsoft-flight-simulator-2020-locations-photos)). Strava's leak through aggregation and default settings ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/01/29/us-military-reviewing-tech-use-after-strava-privacy-snafu)). The Pokémon Go settlement keeping game spots away from family homes ([Law.com](https://law.com/therecorder/2019/02/15/pokemon-go-away-lawsuit-over-once-ubiquitous-mobile-game-settles)). The OpenStreetMap services' app-wide limits ([Nominatim policy](https://operations.osmfoundation.org/policies/nominatim/), [Overpass wiki](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Overpass_API)). All [search excerpts].

**Pillar fit.** 1 and 4: strong. **Possible clash with pillar 5 (Darkly comic, never cruel)** if a link can be used to "shell" a particular person's house and send them the picture, as happened in Flight Simulator. Guardrails: the game, not the player, picks every objective, so there's no way to choose a target building. Escort "homes" are never shown with a house number. There's no public gallery, list or heatmap of shared places, ever.

**Risks.** *Privacy*: **the highest of the five, and this is the flag the role requires: sharing "my neighbourhood" is the game's most powerful hook and its biggest privacy risk.** *Service load*: if a link goes viral and every recipient re-downloads the area, the game could hit the shared OpenStreetMap limits and be blocked. The link should therefore carry or point to a saved map file rather than trigger a fresh download (a question for the Tech Lead). *Scope*: high.

**Smallest test.** Before building anything, show a mock-up of the consent screen to five people and ask them what the link reveals. Build only if they all get it right. Separately, work out on paper how many map-service requests one shared link causes, for 10 and for 10,000 recipients.

## Considered and not proposed

- **Streaks, daily login rewards, "come back or lose it"**: banned by the ethics guardrail, and the PC Gamer critique above shows they turn play into chores.
- **A public map, leaderboard or ranking of places people play**: the Strava lesson. It reveals where players live and turns real suburbs into rankings.
- **In-game rewards for adding to OpenStreetMap ("fill the gap")**: Pokémon Go players added fake parks to the real map to change their game. Because our hordes spawn in OpenStreetMap parks, the game should **never** reward map edits. A neutral, reward-free "this block looks empty on OpenStreetMap; you can help fix it" link, perhaps pointing to the beginner-friendly StreetComplete app ([OSM wiki: StreetComplete](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/StreetComplete) [search excerpt]), could be fine later, but it's not a hook and I didn't rank it.
- **Letting players pick a building as the target**: this invites the Flight Simulator harassment pattern against real households.

## My first pick: idea 1, "Hello, home"

Every other idea depends on one thing: players feeling *it's my place*. PLAN says the whole project rests on that assumption, and "Hello, home" tests it cheaply in phase 2's five-suburb check, using data and systems already planned. It also creates the safest kind of sharing: people tell friends "try it on your street" rather than sending a link to their own. If people don't light up at seeing their school labelled in thermal, we learn that before spending effort on sharing features.

*All ideas here are outside the current Phase 1 backlog and not in `PLAN.md` as written (except that idea 5 builds on the phase 5 links option), so they are class C: the owner decides whether any goes to `/pitch`.*
