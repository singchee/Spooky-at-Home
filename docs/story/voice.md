# Voice: how Ground and Spooky sound

*Story Writer, 21 Nov 2026. Read this before writing any radio line, briefing, coach card or result text.*

## The two characters

**Ground** is the controller on the radio, somewhere below in the dark with a kettle and a map. The player only ever hears Ground.
- **Calm, dry and kind.** Ground has seen a lot of nights. Never panics, never gloats. The only time Ground raises their voice is `CHECK FIRE`.
- **Warm about people, unimpressed by zombies.** Ground cares whether the neighbours get home. The dead get mild contempt and paperwork humour, not hatred.
- **Understated, British/Australian.** "Kettle's on." "Well, try." "Textbook." Ground says less than they feel.
- **Honest.** Ground never bluffs, never exaggerates and never says "unconfirmed" (intel is always true, PLAN §5a). If Ground says it, it's so.
- **Running bits:** the kettle (always on, never quite drunk); the residents' committee (fictional, permanently about to complain about the lawns); "Spooky, go home" at the very end.

**Spooky** is the gunship's callsign, and the player is its gunner. Spooky has **no voice**: the player is Spooky, and putting words in their mouth would break that. Ground talks *to* Spooky, as one tired professional to another: "Spooky, Ground." / "Textbook, Spooky." Ground can tease Spooky gently (the lawns, going quiet) and is always on their side.

New characters (a loader calling reloads, a pilot) need the owner's approval first.

## How a line is built

- **Under 12 words,** one idea, the punchline last. Two short sentences beat one long one.
- **Callsign first when it's an order or a warning** ("Spooky, Ground."), last when it's warmth ("Textbook, Spooky.").
- **Plain words.** Gunship terms only where they're used correctly: *cleared hot* (you may fire), *check fire* (stop firing now), *danger close* (friendlies near the impact), *good effect* (the rounds did what was wanted).
- **Exclamation marks only for P1 safety calls.** Everything else is full stops.
- **Recorded lines have no slots.** Names and numbers go in the on-screen text only (`radio-script.md`, "Rules for every line").

## What the jokes are about

| Yes | No |
|---|---|
| The zombies' manners, timing and lack of awareness ("Nobody told them.") | Survivors, the hurt or the dead as people |
| Suburban life in general: the kettle, committees, self-checkouts, sport | A real, named place, person, business, club or group as the butt. Real names only appear as plain facts in briefings |
| The crew's own job: the lawns, the craters, going quiet | Any school, kindergarten, childcare or playground. They're not in the game at all |
| Understatement under pressure ("Doors won't take much more of that.") | Gore, cruelty, or relish in killing |
| A little bleakness that respects loss ("Not everyone made it tonight.") | Guilt trips, blame, or punishing the player for being away |

A good test: **would the line still be fine if it played over the player's own street, with their neighbours listening?** If not, cut it.

## Words to use and avoid

- **Use:** *contacts, the dead, hordes, waves* (zombies); *survivors, friendlies, stragglers, neighbours, passengers, shoppers* (people); *safehouse*; *cold-blooded / running cold*; *home*.
- **Avoid:** *targets* or *civilians* for people (cold and clinical); *kills* in voiced lines (Ground says "down"); *kids, children, school*; anything naming a real organisation (for example "Neighbourhood Watch") as a punchline.

## Delivery notes for the audio director

- One voice for Ground everywhere: "Robert - Measured Thriller Narrator", the same as the existing 20 lines.
- **Default:** low, level, unhurried, a half-smile on the jokes. Don't sell the punchline; Ground wouldn't.
- **P1 lines** (`check-fire`, `ff-one-left`): clipped and firm, not shouted. Urgency comes from pace, not volume.
- **Loss lines** (`night-won-losses`, `survivor-lost`): quieter, a beat slower, no joke in the voice.
- **The ending** (`n3-close`): the warmest line in the game. Tired, fond, slightly relieved.
- No radio effects baked in: the game adds the squelch.

## Truth checklist (every new line)

1. Which sim field or event makes it true? Write it next to the line.
2. Is it true **every** time it can play, at every difficulty and on every map, including substitutes and fallbacks?
3. Does it name a place or a number? Then it's on-screen only, and the slot comes from mission or sim data.
4. Does it rely on a feature that isn't merged yet? Mark it **Hold** until it is.
5. Does it pass the "over the player's own street" test above?
